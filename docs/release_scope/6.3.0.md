## Why these lines are here and not in 6.2.0

Two moves put them here, for two different reasons, and each section says which one it belongs to.

On 2026-09-23 the recast of 6.2.0 placed its lines against the four outcomes: E1 a second
implementation, E2 in-toto PR 575, E3 three external users, E4 adoption into a specification. A line
belonged there when it brought one of them closer, closed a P1 of the audit of 2026-09-19, or closed
a place where the verifier says more than it checks. The 34 rows from "Mutation gate and coverage"
to "Cleaning up" did none of those, and each carries the sentence saying so, as the release rule of
2026-09-09 requires: a register line per move, not a bulk transfer.

On 2026-09-27 the owner cut 6.2.0 to what is on `main` and the frozen fixes of that release, and
every other line of it came here. Those 14 rows are in the first section below. They stood in 6.2.0
BECAUSE they carry an outward outcome, so "carries no outward outcome" would be false for them, and
their ledger column says why they moved instead.

All of them are open, not dismissed; `RESTRISIKO_610.md` names those among its 54 and continues to.

the source list wrote them. `Z.715 / Z.728` carries two printed identifiers of which the list names

rows of 2026-09-27 carry 12 of the 54: fourteen, minus three new ones, plus one for `C1 / N15`.

**On 2026-09-24 two rows traded files after a counter-reading, and the two numbers of that day did
not move.** R1 went to 6.2.0 because the parity registry ships in the sdist and reaches a second
implementer, and N3-5 came here so the binding N3 chain is not executed backwards. Both are in the
list of 54, so 35 and 18 each stayed where they were. A swap that changed those numbers would have
meant a line had been lost or invented on the way, which is exactly what the sum is there to catch.
R1 has since landed on `main` and stays in 6.2.0.

**Deviation from the template of 2026-09-23, stated rather than smoothed.** That order named 31
lines for this file. Counted by the rows of the quoted tables of 2026-09-19, it is **34**. The
arithmetic closes against 54 either way, so the difference is in how grouped rows are counted, not
in a lost line: `A5.1–A5.4`, `S59/S60/S65`, `N1-2a–2c`, `N2-3a–d` and `R3/R4` are ONE row each in
the source list and are kept as one row here. No line was dropped, and none was invented.
