# The policy-boundary matrix: rounds 1 and 2 derived, round 3 on hold

This directory holds the policy-boundary matrix over the differential corpus: one row per registered
vector, 64 rows so far, derived by `../policy_matrix.py` from the stored bytes of rounds 1 and 2
alone. Round 3's vectors are not registered: their design goes through an external review first
(owner hold of 2026-09-27, 21:0x Berlin), and its rows join this matrix when they are measured.

## WHAT THE MATRIX SEPARATES

A reviewer of the first two rounds asked to see two things apart:

1. which bytes the service commits to in data-hash (columns "data-hash match" and "committed
   representation");
2. which semantically awkward or conflicting COSE structures its parser and policy still accept
   (columns "protected semantics", "unprotected semantics", "accepted/refused", "parser-visible
   interpretation").

The question behind it: is a10 (label 15, CWT claims, in both buckets, with a conflicting issuer in the
unprotected one, accepted) an isolated permissive case or part of a reproducible class?

## PINS AND LIMITS

- The service, as recorded per vector in `../differential_corpus/` and `../differential_corpus_round2/`:
  https://github.com/microsoft/scitt-ccf-ledger at `00101f769d872711356e080fbb089ac48589c60a`,
  image `sha256:1bdd60edc1b8cfc1fb02ed516b5a5ea60d75a3ca4b5d59f128e3c61e23680380`, CCF `ccf-7.0.17`.
- Local, one node, virtual mode (attestation format `Insecure_Virtual`), no TEE; one service commit.
- One signer per round (a P-256 CA and leaf made for that round; `did:x509` issuer pinned to the CA).
- Configuration: the first round's policy ("any statement with a CWT issuer",
  `scitt governance local_development`); round 2 adds two later phases (a policy change and a restart),
  each with its own key set.
- Rows are derived here offline; nothing was registered for this matrix.

## THE COLUMNS

The reviewer's eight columns, then two of ours.

| column | what it holds | origin |
|---|---|---|
| vector | round and vector id | the corpus |
| protected semantics | the protected header as submitted, entry by entry in the submitted order, every encoding that is not the preferred one named (`<1-byte argument, NOT shortest>`, `<indefinite length>`) | the corpus's own CBOR reader (`differential_corpus.cose_structure`) on the stored request bytes |
| unprotected semantics | the unprotected header as submitted, the same way | as above |
| accepted/refused | what the service did, nothing else | fixture: the recorded service answer |
| data-hash match | for an accepted vector, whether the receipt's data-hash equals the preimage rule, through three encoders; `n/a` for a refused one | own computation (`preimage_candidates.py`, candidate 4-tagged); foreign library `cbor2` re-encoding the same candidate; foreign tool built by us (`vendored_encoder_probe.py`, CCF's vendored EverCBOR encoder) |
| committed representation | for an accepted vector, the bytes the data-hash commits to: tag 18, the protected header, an empty unprotected map, the payload and the signature, each as submitted or not, and the labels of the returned statement's unprotected map | the preimage rule and the stored returned statement |
| parser-visible interpretation | only what can be observed without changing the service: the error text of a refusal; otherwise NOT MEASURABLE, with the reason | fixture: the recorded service answer |
| expected-policy result | the normative source for the row | PENDING: it comes with the reviewed design; no conformance claim is made either way |
| our reader, submitted | `src/proofbundle/scitt_ccf.py` (scitt-ccf/v1) on the exact submitted bytes: the whole check's status (always `malformed` here, a Signed Statement carries no receipt), the statement side's status, the decode status (the column rounds 1 and 2 recorded) and the statement signature's status, then the reason as the reader gives it | own reader, run on the stored bytes |
| our reader, returned | the same reader on the exact returned statement, `verify_transparent_statement` with the round's service key set and signer key | own reader, run on the stored bytes |

`matrix.json` carries more per row than the table: the service's status and txid, each data-hash path
separately, the receipt's data-hash and whether it equals the round's control, the reader's full
output, whether emptying the unprotected map changes our reader's verdict
(`reader_unprotected_effect`, a variant derived here, the first round's candidate 11 form), and our
reader beside the service (`reader_against_service`, measured, not judged).

## WHAT THE ROWS SHOW

Measurement:

- Data-hash: all 48 accepted rows match the preimage rule through all three encoders.
- The same data-hash as the round's control, in 34 of the 48:
  - every accepted mutation of the unprotected bucket: a01 to a13 (a10 among them), e01 to e03, and
    h05 to h09, where a label stands in both buckets, equal or conflicting (alg, the payload hash
    algorithm, CWT claims, x5chain);
  - the outer re-encodings b01, b02, c01 to c03 and d01 to d03, stored in their shortest form;
  - the control's own bytes again (control-resubmitted, j01, j02).
- In each of those rows the returned statement's unprotected map holds only label 394. The committed
  representation carries no unprotected label, so the conflicting issuer of a10 and the conflicting
  alg of h06 are not in the committed bytes.
- Refusals: all 16 carry an error text, the parser-visible interpretation of these rows:
  - `QCBOR_ERR_DUPLICATE_LABEL` for a duplicate label in the protected header;
  - `Signature verification failed` for a duplicate in the unprotected map and for an indefinite
    protected map;
  - the crit texts for unknown or empty crit.
- Our reader against the service: 24 rows stricter than the service, 0 less strict. The service
  accepted, and our reader's statement side says `malformed` or `outside_profile`:
  - non-preferred heads: b, c, d, g03 to g08;
  - a label in both buckets: a09, a10, h05 to h09;
  - crit that scitt-ccf/v1 does not process: i01 to i03.
- Our reader and the unprotected bucket:
  - emptying the unprotected map changes our reader's statement-side verdict in 9 rows (a09, a10,
    h03 to h09), each time from `malformed` to `confirmed`, that is, the unprotected content only
    ever leads to a refusal;
  - in no row does a value from the unprotected bucket lead our reader to accept or confirm.
- Our reader on the returned statement: `confirmed` for a09, a10 and h05 to h09. Their returned
  statements no longer carry the unprotected label, so the reader confirms bytes without the conflict.

Not judged here: whether any of these rows should have been refused. That needs the normative source
per row, which the reviewed design brings.

## NOT MEASURABLE

- Parser-visible interpretation, 48 accepted rows. The service accepted, and what it returned (the
  statement, the receipt) does not say which of the submitted header values its parser or policy read.
  Rounds 1 and 2 queried no other read endpoint, and observing it otherwise needs a change to the
  service.
- Expected-policy result, 64 rows: PENDING, not measurable before the reviewed design names a source per row.
