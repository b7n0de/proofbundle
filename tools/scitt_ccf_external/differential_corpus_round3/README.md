# The policy-boundary matrix: rounds 1, 2 and 3, design version 2

This directory holds round 3 of the differential corpus and the policy-boundary matrix over all three
rounds: one row per registered vector, 84 rows, derived by `../policy_matrix.py` from stored bytes. Round
3 was registered on 2026-09-27 in a new frozen cohort, on a scitt-ccf-ledger rebuilt for it, following
design version 2 of the owner order of that day: reruns of the control, a10, h08, h07 and h06; five
core mutations with their controls; and the two-key x5chain experiment. The diagnostic policy probe was
not run. Rows of rounds 1 and 2 keep their own pins and are never combined with round 3's.

## WHAT THE MATRIX SEPARATES

1. Which bytes the service commits to in data-hash (data-hash rule match, reconstructed commitment
   preimage).
2. Which semantically awkward or conflicting COSE structures its parser and policy accept (P and U
   semantics, final registration outcome, interpretation observation).
3. What the named sources say about each row (normative class and source), kept apart from what was
   predicted and what was measured.

`matrix.json` carries more per row: the machine record of round 3 (generator and reader, service pins,
configuration digest and transaction, external AAD, the control, the signer, every HTTP exchange's
status and body digest, file digests), the reader booleans on the submitted bytes, whether emptying the
unprotected map changes our reader's verdict (`reader_unprotected_effect`), and our reader beside the
service (`reader_against_service`, measured, not judged).

## SOURCES

`sources.json` names the revision of every cited source: the WG repository, the tag and commit (or why
there is no tag), the file, its size and SHA-256, the retrieval time, and how each cited section maps to
a heading of the source. The published RFC texts were NOT RETRIEVED: www.rfc-editor.org and www.ietf.org
are not reachable from the measuring environment; the drafts are the last revisions the WG repositories
hold, and three RFCs have no tag for their last draft (named there). Every rule quoted in
`normative.json` was found verbatim in its archived file (24 quotes). The archived files themselves are
not committed.

## WHAT ROUND 3 SHOWS

Measurement:

- Every outcome of round 3 equals its prediction: 14 registered, 6 refused, each refusal at `POST
  /entries` with HTTP 400. No timeout, no unfinished operation, no receipt that failed to verify.
- The reruns a10, h08, h07 and h06 were registered in the new cohort as in rounds 1 and 2, each with the
  control's data-hash; so was B259-conflicting. The returned statements of all 14 carry only label 394
  unprotected, and each protected header comes back as submitted.
- Data-hash: all 14 registered rows match the preimage rule through own computation and cbor2.
- M-u15, CWT Claims moved to U: refused, "Signed statement protected header must contain CWT_Claims
  with at least an issuer". M-u258 and M-u259 (258 or 259 moved to U) and M-u2 (crit moved to U, 33 kept
  in P) were registered.
- The two-key experiment. A-only and B-only were registered. With x5chain in both buckets:

  | P x5chain | U x5chain | signed with | signature verifies under | service |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | A | B | A | A | registered |
  | A | B | B | B | refused, "Signature verification failed" |
  | B | A | A | A | refused, "Signature verification failed" |
  | B | A | B | B | registered |

  Of four hypotheses, the outcomes refute three: that the service selects the key from the unprotected
  x5chain when one is present (it would have registered the second and third rows), that it tries the
  chains of both buckets (it would have registered all four), and that it refuses an x5chain in both
  buckets (it would have refused all four). They are consistent with the fourth, that the key is the
  protected x5chain's. NOT MEASURABLE from these outcomes: whether the service reads the unprotected
  x5chain at all, for example to check it and then let the protected one govern, as RFC 9052 section 3
  asks of a message that is not rejected.

Judgement, per row in `normative.json`: M-u15, M-u258, M-u259, B259-conflicting and M-u2 each violate
a mandatory input constraint of a named source (RFC 9943 section 6; RFC 9995 section 4; RFC 9052
section 3.1), so do the reruns a10, h08 (RFC 9597 section 2) and h07 (RFC 9995 section 4). The service
registered seven of those nine. The sources make none of these an explicit rejection requirement.

## OUR READER (addendum of 20:4x)

- Rounds 1 and 2: unchanged, 24 rows stricter than the service, none less strict.
- Round 3: 11 rows stricter than the service, and one less strict: M-u15. The service refused it, and
  our reader's statement side read the submitted bytes as `confirmed`, because the v1 profile did not
  require CWT Claims in the protected header. That is a finding against PR 278 (a named source requires
  what the reader did not check). Red test and fix, both on their own branch from PR 278's head
  `531e2564`: `claude/scitt-reader-cwt-claims`, red `1f07ccf3`, fix `56061d8d`. They are not on PR 278:
  the owner decides whether this profile change lands there.
- Emptying the unprotected map changes our reader's statement-side verdict in 11 round 3 rows: in 9 from
  `malformed` or `outside_profile` to `confirmed`, in the two refused two-key rows from `malformed` to
  `statement_signature_invalid`. In no row does the unprotected content lead our reader to accept. On
  the returned statements our reader confirms 12 of the 14 registered rows; `c-crit33` and M-u258 stay
  `outside_profile` (crit that v1 does not process; 258 absent from P).

## NOT MEASURABLE, NOT MEASURED

- Interpretation observation, 58 registered rows outside the two-key experiment: the service accepted,
  and what it returned does not say which submitted header values its parser or policy read.
- Data-hash rule match and commitment preimage, 22 refused rows: no receipt.
- EverCBOR path, 14 registered rows of round 3: NOT MEASURED, the vendored encoder probe needs a CCF
  clone at `ccf-7.0.17` and an EverParse build and was not run in this step.
- Predicted outcome, 64 rows of rounds 1 and 2: NOT RECORDED before they were measured.
- The transaction that wrote the configuration: NOT MEASURED.

## LIMITS

Local, one node, virtual mode, no TEE; one service commit; one signer per round (round 3: one CA, two
leaves). Nothing here says how another Transparency Service, another configuration or another version
behaves.
